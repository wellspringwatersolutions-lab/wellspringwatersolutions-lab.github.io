Town water or a well?

  • Most homes in Woodstown borough get town water. The borough runs its own water system from five wells. It serves the borough and some set areas of Pilesgrove and Mannington (state report on Woodstown utilities).
  • Many homes in Pilesgrove have their own private well. State records show 185 Pilesgrove wells were tested at home sales from 2002 to 2007 (NJ DEP well test results, 2002–2007).
  • Radium is not common here. Only 1 of those 185 Pilesgrove wells was over the state limit for gross alpha. It’s a different story a few miles east in Pittsgrove and Upper Pittsgrove, which the state health department lists as high-radium areas (NJ Department of Health radium flyer).
  • What we see most in local wells is the everyday stuff: orange iron stains, blue-green stains from acidic water, rotten egg smell, and hard water scale.

In our six counties, New Jersey’s home-sale well test also checks for gross alpha (a sign of radium) and mercury, on top of the standard tests (NJ DEP well testing list). Every well is different, so we start with a free in-home water test.

Questions we hear in Woodstown & Pilesgrove

I live in Woodstown borough. Do I have a well?

Most likely not. Most homes in the borough get town water. Town water can still be hard, and some people want better-tasting drinking water. A softener or a reverse osmosis system at the sink can help.

I’m selling a home in Pilesgrove. What do I need?

If the home has a private well, New Jersey requires a certified lab to test it before closing (NJ DEP Private Well Testing Act page). If the test finds a problem, we can help fix it. See selling a home with a well.
