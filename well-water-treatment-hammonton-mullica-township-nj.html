Town water and wells

  • Hammonton has town water. The Hammonton Water Department is a public system that pumps from its own wells (NJ DEP report on Hammonton’s water). Homes outside the water system use private wells.
  • Mullica Township has many wells. From 2002 to 2007, 308 Mullica Township wells were tested at home sales, and 19% were over the state limit for gross alpha, a sign of radium (NJ DEP well test results, 2002–2007).
  • Neighbors are on the state’s list. The NJ Department of Health names Mullica, Buena Vista, and Folsom as Atlantic County towns with a high share of wells over the limit (NJ Department of Health radium flyer). In the older data, 38% of 109 Hammonton wells tested were over, but newer results should be checked on the NJ DEP well test maps.

The state health department says a water softener or reverse osmosis system can lower radium in well water, and that these systems need upkeep to keep working. In homes it checked in three South Jersey towns, 27% of treatment systems were not bringing radioactivity down to safe levels, likely because of poor maintenance (NJ Department of Health radium flyer). My well water leaves blue-green stains. Why?

That usually means acidic water wearing on copper pipes. It’s common in South Jersey wells. An acid neutralizer fixes it.
