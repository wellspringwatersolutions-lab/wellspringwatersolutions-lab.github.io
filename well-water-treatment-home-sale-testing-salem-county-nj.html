Selling a home with a well in Salem County

  • The well must be tested before closing. New Jersey requires a certified lab to test a private well when the home is sold, and both buyer and seller get the results (NJ DEP Private Well Testing Act page).
  • Salem County tests include radium and mercury. In our six counties, New Jersey’s home-sale well test also checks for gross alpha (a sign of radium) and mercury, on top of the standard tests (NJ DEP well testing list).
  • The test isn’t cheap. The NJ Department of Health lists the average cost of the required test at about $1,250 (NJ DOH private well fact sheet). Our free in-home water test is not that official lab test.

If the lab test turns up a problem, it doesn’t have to stop the sale. We go over the results with you in plain language and recommend only what the water needs.

Where radium shows up

Across Salem County, 14% of wells tested from 2002 to 2007 were over the state limit for gross alpha (NJ DEP well test results, 2002–2007). It’s not even across the county. The NJ Department of Health lists Pittsgrove, Upper Pittsgrove, Alloway, and Oldmans as high-radium towns (NJ Department of Health radium flyer), while Pilesgrove had only 1 of 185 wells over. Many homes in Woodstown, Salem City, and Pennsville are on public water.

Questions we hear in Salem County

Who does the official home-sale well test?

A New Jersey certified lab. The state keeps the list of certified labs (NJ DEP Private Well Testing Act page). We help after the test, with treatment.

What if the test fails right before closing?

Call us as soon as you have results. Many problems, like iron, acidic water, or radium, have standard fixes.

Do landlords have to test too?

Yes. New Jersey requires landlords to test well water for tenants every five years (NJ DOH fact sheet).
