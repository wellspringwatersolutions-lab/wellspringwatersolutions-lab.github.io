What the state data shows

  • More than half the wells tested were high. From 2002 to 2007, 56% of Upper Deerfield wells and 52% of Hopewell Township wells tested at home sales were over the state limit for gross alpha. Deerfield was 43% (NJ DEP well test results, 2002–2007).
  • Still on the state’s list. The NJ Department of Health names Deerfield, Fairfield, Greenwich, Hopewell, Stow Creek, and Upper Deerfield as Cumberland County towns with a high share of wells over the limit (NJ Department of Health radium flyer).
  • Radium comes from the ground. The health department says the main South Jersey aquifer, the Kirkwood-Cohansey, naturally has high radium. You can’t see, smell, or taste it (NJ Department of Health radium flyer).

The state health department says a water softener or reverse osmosis system can lower radium in well water, and that these systems need upkeep to keep working. In homes it checked in three South Jersey towns, 27% of treatment systems were not bringing radioactivity down to safe levels, likely because of poor maintenance (NJ Department of Health radium flyer). Questions we hear in Upper Deerfield & Hopewell

What does “gross alpha” on my well test mean?

It measures radioactivity in the water. In South Jersey, it mostly comes from radium. A high number means you should treat your water.

What’s the usual fix?

Often a water softener for the whole house, plus reverse osmosis at the kitchen sink for drinking water. We size it to your test.

How often should I retest after treatment?

We recommend a follow-up test after install, then checking regularly, since a system that isn’t maintained can stop working.
