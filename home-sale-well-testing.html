Selling a Home with a Well?

If you're selling a home in New Jersey that gets its drinking water from a private well, the well water has to be tested. If the test turns up a problem, we can help you fix it.

What New Jersey requires

New Jersey's Private Well Testing Act requires that when a property that gets its drinking water from a private well is sold, the well water must be tested by a state-certified lab, and both the buyer and seller must receive and review the results before closing. Many leased properties with private wells must also be tested (every five years). The required tests include things like bacteria, nitrate, iron, manganese, pH, lead, arsenic, and certain PFAS.

  • Testing is done by a New Jersey certified laboratory
  • Both buyer and seller review the results before closing
  • For details, see the NJ DEP Private Well Testing Act page

Did the test find a problem?

A test result that doesn't meet a standard doesn't have to stop your sale. We help homeowners fix the problems well tests find, like iron, acidic water, or contaminants such as PFAS, arsenic, or nitrate.
